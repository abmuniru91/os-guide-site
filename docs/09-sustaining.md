# 9. Sustaining the Open-Source Guide

## 9.1 What to Contribute

This guide is intended to be a living document. The field of digital inclusion for women entrepreneurs in sub-Saharan Africa is moving quickly. User behaviours are changing as smartphone penetration increases, the product landscape is evolving, and local language technology is improving. What holds true today about connectivity constraints may look different in a few years. The value of this guide over time depends on whether new practitioners engage with it, challenge it, and update it with their own evidence.

The most useful contributions are specific and evidence-based. If your project confirmed something in this guide, say so. If your findings contradict something, say that too, and explain the context — region, product type, and user demographics all matter. Priority contribution areas:

- New case examples from similar projects in Ghana or elsewhere in the region.
- Updated data on connectivity, smartphone ownership, or digital literacy patterns.
- Additional screening templates, observation guides, or evaluation frameworks adapted for specific contexts.
- Lessons from product types or user groups not covered in this edition.
- Additional Phase 2 solutions and later Phase 2 cohorts not yet reflected in Section 4, Part B and Section 5.6.
- Translations of this guide or its appendices into local languages.
- Design patterns or component libraries tested with this user population.

## 9.2 Contribution Guidelines

Contributions to this guide should be practical and based on documented field activity, not general design theory; clearly referenced, stating what project, context, and evidence base the contribution draws on; aligned with user-centred design principles and gender-responsive practice; and submitted with transparency about limitations, presenting context-specific findings as such.

## 9.3 Review and Governance Model

Ownership of this guide is intended to transfer from the GFA/GIZ DTEG project team to a Ghana Hubs Network (GHN) open-source community as part of the project's sustainability plan. Proposed contributions should be reviewed by a designated editorial group with expertise in UX/UI design, gender-responsive development, and digital inclusion in sub-Saharan Africa — drawn from GHN member organizations once the hand-over is complete. Major updates should follow versioned releases; minor corrections and additions can be incorporated in interim updates. The review process should prioritize evidence quality, contextual accuracy, and practical relevance. Contributions that add theoretical framework without field evidence should be directed to a supplementary resources section rather than the main guide.

## 9.4 Knowledge-Sharing Platforms

This guide is intended to be distributed and built upon through a Git-based repository (GitHub or GitLab) for version-controlled collaborative editing; GHN community forums and practitioner networks focused on digital inclusion and gender-responsive design; workshops, learning sessions, and design clinics organized under GHN's knowledge-sharing agenda; and partner organization publications and distribution channels.

## 9.6 Long-Term Hosting and Maintenance

For the guide to remain accessible and maintainable after the DTEG project closes, it needs a stable home independent of any single organization's internal file storage. Two options are worth GHN and GIZ deciding between: (1) a dedicated page or subdomain on GHN's own institutional website, giving GHN full editorial and hosting control but requiring GHN to commit web-maintenance capacity; or (2) a public Git repository (for example on GitHub), which supports version history and community contribution natively and costs nothing to host, but requires at least one GHN-side maintainer comfortable with that workflow. A hybrid — a Git repository as the source of truth, mirrored to a readable page on GHN's site — captures the benefits of both and is the most common pattern for open-source documentation projects of this kind.

## 9.7 Sustaining This as a Revenue Stream for GHN Members

Section 8 sets out how individual hubs can turn the capability this guide documents into a paid service. From a network-sustainability perspective, that is also what keeps this guide alive in practice: hubs that are actively running user testing engagements are the ones most likely to generate the new case studies, corrections, and Phase 2 content this guide will need (Section 9.1). Treat Section 8 and this section as two sides of the same goal — one hub's income, and the whole network's evidence base.

## 9.8 Applying the Guide Flexibly

No two projects are identical. The guidance here comes from a specific context: Ghana, 2023–2025, peri-urban and rural women micro-entrepreneurs. Before applying any recommendation elsewhere, ask what is the same and what is different. The principles tend to transfer; the specific implementation details often need adjustment.
